Stage benches localize a fault. Two things were missing: real data inside the benches, and
evidence that a fix localized in a bench also fixes the system. Capture supplies the first; the
loop checks supply the second.

## Real runs become fixtures (#446)

Any moment of a run can be replayed in its stage's bench and debugged at unit-test speed. The
backend reports its stage boundaries to an optional **tap**; a **recorder** turns each one it
selects into a `captured` fixture, holding the stage's input and the output the run produced.

```text
MsckfBackend ──set_stage_tap(&recorder)──▶ StageRecorder<T> ──▶ DIR/<stage>/<name>.json
 (sdk: stage_tap.hpp)                       (tools: stage_recorder.hpp)        │
                                                                               ▼
                                              s6d_gating_bench --fixture f000013_S6d_gating_feat127.json
```

### From the command line

`vio_pipeline` captures from the synthetic world or from EuRoC:

```bash
# every boundary of post-init frame 13 (the frame numbering starts at initialization)
./build/tools/vio_pipeline --capture cap/ --capture-frames 13:13

# EuRoC: only the S6 updates whose NIS/dof exceeds 5, every one through S5 → S6e
./build/tools/vio_pipeline --source euroc --dataset .../MH_05_difficult/mav0 \
    --capture cap/ --capture-nis 5

# the first boundary, anywhere in the run, whose fixtures fail their bench
./build/tools/vio_pipeline --capture cap/ --capture-first-violation

# then replay one in its bench: every type, every variant, in seconds
./build/tools/s5_triangulation_bench --fixture cap/S5_triangulation/f000002_S5_triangulation_feat19.json
```

`--capture-stages S2_propagation,S6d_gating` narrows the stages and `--capture-max N` caps the
count. In C++, set a `StageRecorder<T>` on any `MsckfBackend<T>` with `set_stage_tap`.

### What is captured

| Backend boundary | Fixtures (one bench each) | Expected output |
|---|---|---|
| S0, each frame and camera | `S0_sensor_model`: its pixels and intrinsics | the run's bearings and validity |
| S2, each IMU step and the hold to the frame time | `S2_propagation`: state, sample, noise, gravity | the run's state after |
| S3 | `S3_augmentation`: state, clone time | the run's state after |
| S6, each feature track | `S5_triangulation`, `S6a_jacobians`, `S6b_nullspace_projection`, `S6c_compression`, `S6d_gating`, `S6e_ekf_update`: as far as the track got | the run's gate decision, NIS and covariance after; the intermediates recomputed |
| S9 | `S9_marginalization`: state, clone index | the run's state after |

The transformations themselves are not stored as matrices. Each bench rebuilds them from the
captured input, bit for bit: F/Φ and Q_d (S2), the clone Jacobian (S3), H_x, H_f and r (S6a), the
projected H₀ and r₀ (S6b), S (S6d), K and δx (S6e). The intermediates the run hands from one S6
sub-step to the next (p_f, the measurement system, the projected system) are captured as the next
sub-step's input. The state before and after every stage is in the fixture: nav state, biases,
clones, calibration and P.

The S6 intermediates are not exposed by the backend, so the recorder recomputes the chain with the
same stage functions. It then checks the chain against the run: the outcome, the NIS and the state
after must equal the run's bit for bit. A difference is counted and reported, never hidden.

Each fixture is tagged with the arithmetic the run used and the platform it was captured on, so
`replay.bit_identical` holds there. Not captured: S1 (initialization), S4 (the frontend: the
backend never sees images; `asl_trace` covers S4) and S10. A multi-camera updater's S6 boundaries
are skipped, because the S5 and S6a benches model one camera.

### What selects a capture

- **The stage:** any of the fixture stages above.
- **A frame range:** post-initialization frames, inclusive. S2 steps belong to the frame they
  propagate to.
- **A trigger:**
  - **NIS:** an S6 update whose NIS per degree of freedom exceeds a threshold;
  - **first violation:** the first boundary whose fixtures fail their bench (invariants and replay).

  With a trigger set, a boundary is captured only when a trigger fires on it.

### Cost when off

The tap is a pointer; off, each stage boundary costs one null test, and nothing is copied. A tap
that wants a boundary is the only thing that copies the state. Measured on the synthetic world
(250 frames, Release):

| Run | Wall time (median of 7) | Outputs |
|---|---|---|
| `main`, before the tap existed | 1.2690 s | — |
| this branch, capture off | 1.2692 s | `trajectory.csv`, `run.jsonl` and stdout byte-identical to `main` |

With capture on (single runs, same world):

| Selection | Wall time | Captured |
|---|---|---|
| `--capture-frames 13:13` (every stage, one frame) | 1.28 s | 49 fixtures, 19 boundaries, 11 MB |
| `--capture-nis 5` | 2.15 s | 215 fixtures, 43 S6 updates, 31 MB |
| `--capture-first-violation` | 1.28 s | 1 fixture (frame 2, S5) |
| `--capture-stages S9_marginalization` | 1.54 s | 239 fixtures, 82 MB |

A test (`stage_capture`) also checks that a tap which captures nothing, or every stage, leaves the
filter's final state and NIS bit-identical.

### What capture found

Run on the synthetic world with `--capture-first-violation`, the first frames after initialization
turned up two things the stage benches' own fixtures had not:

- **S5 rejects tracks whose rays diverge, and its contract calls that a failure.** A two-view track
  at the image edge (0.74° of parallax, normal-matrix κ ≈ 2.4e4) with ordinary pixel noise has rays
  that meet 6 cm *behind* the cameras. Every method rejects it: the shipped solve, the midpoint, the
  DLT and inverse depth. The bench's `triangulation.resolved` exempts only a parallax gate, so a
  correct rejection reads as a violation. It is the run's first violation, within three frames of
  initialization.
- **The S6d symmetry check was too tight.** The bench rebuilds S = H·P·Hᵀ + σ²I and held its
  asymmetry to the scale of S (σ² ≈ 1e-4). The products in H·P·Hᵀ cancel, so their rounding scales
  with |H|·|P|·|H|ᵀ instead; a real update showed 3.8e-18. The bench now scales the tolerance by the
  products' magnitude.

S0 capture also runs into the synthetic camera's p₂, which is 1000× EuRoC's
([#467](https://github.com/branes-ai/cortex/issues/467)). The recorder therefore takes the
backend's camera through `intrinsics_of`, never a copy of EuRoC's values.

## The loop is checked as a loop (#447)

Some failures only emerge from stages running together, and over-confidence is the main example.
The stage benches localize a fault; this layer confirms that a fix fixes the system. It has three
parts: the composition bench, live assertion, and a trajectory-level report.

### The composition bench

`c_filter_loop_bench` runs the loop itself: a **tape** of stage operations in the order the backend
ran them, from a starting state, through the shipped stage functions.

```text
S2 … S2 → [S6 per track on the oldest clone → S9] → S3 → S6 per ended track → S2 …
```

At every operation, that operation's own stage contract is checked: the stage bench's invariants,
evaluated on the loop's input and output for it. A fault is therefore attributed to the stage that
introduced it. The report names each result `<stage>.<invariant>`, puts the first violation first,
and gives its operation index (`loop.first_violating_op`).

Loop-level results cover what no single stage owns:

| Result | What it is |
|---|---|
| `loop.covariance_symmetric`, `loop.covariance_psd` | the covariance the loop ends with |
| `loop.unobservable_leak_max`, `_mean` | per applied update, the share of the information it adds along the four unobservable directions, ‖H₀N̂‖²/‖H₀‖² |
| `loop.nis_per_dof` | over the loop's updates |
| `truth.nav_nees` | ground truth: the final nav state's NEES (15 dof), bound χ²₀.₉₉₉ = 37.7 |

Its fixtures are the three kinds every bench has:

| Fixture | Input | Judged against |
|---|---|---|
| `known_answer_constant_velocity` | constant velocity, exact observations, P₀ = 0, Q = 0: 68 operations through S2, S3, S5–S6e and S9 | the mean follows the motion exactly, P stays 0, every NIS is 0 |
| `ground_truth_synthetic_three_frames` | a synthetic run's tape (post-init frames 16–18), its starting mean reset to truth | NEES of the final nav state |
| `captured_synthetic_three_frames` | the same tape from the run's own state | the run's final state and per-update NIS, bit for bit |

All three pass in double, float, `posit<32,2>` and long double. The sweep runs 1 to 16 cycles.

**Injected faults are caught where they are injected.** Two variants inject a defect into one stage:

- `fault_s3_clone_jacobian` perturbs the new clone's cross-covariance. The first violation is
  `S3_augmentation.augmentation.blocks`, at the first S3 operation.
- `fault_s6a_jacobian` scales a clone-rotation block of H_x by 1.5. The first violation is
  `S6a_jacobians.jacobian.state_vs_fd`, the finite-difference check, at the first S6 operation.

**Capture a loop from any run.** `LoopTape<T>` is a stage tap (#446) that records the tape:

```bash
./build/tools/vio_pipeline --source euroc --dataset .../V1_01_easy/mav0 --capture cap/ --capture-loop 100:102
./build/tools/c_filter_loop_bench --fixture cap/C_filter_loop/loop.json
```

A V1_01 loop over frames 100–102 (38 operations) replays bit for bit and satisfies every stage and
loop invariant.

### Live assertion

`vio_pipeline --live-assert` checks every stage boundary of a full run against its bench, as it
happens. It reports the first violation: stage, frame, invariant, value and bound. With `--capture`
it also writes that boundary as a fixture.

```text
live assertion  : FIRST VIOLATION S5_triangulation, frame 4 (t=1.40372e+09 s), triangulation.resolved = 0 (bound 1)
```

It is the stage recorder (#446) with the first-violation trigger, so nothing new runs on the
filter's path. A normal build never sets a tap, and its outputs are byte-identical to `main`. On
V1_01 and on the synthetic world, the first violation is the S5 contract problem filed as
[#487](https://github.com/branes-ai/cortex/issues/487): a two-view track whose rays diverge,
correctly rejected and still counted as a failure. Until #487 is fixed, live assertion stops there.

A test checks the attribution on a running filter. A fault injected into S3 at post-init frame 0
is reported as `S3_augmentation`, frame 0.

### Trajectory-level consistency on EuRoC

`vio_trajectory_report --dataset <mav0> --label NAME --out DIR` runs the MSCKF over a sequence and
writes `NAME_report.json` and per-window `NAME_windows.csv`. It reports:

- **NEES**, gauge-anchored at the first post-init frame, overall and per block;
- **NIS** over all updates;
- **the update leak**, using the stage tap to recompute each applied update's H₀;
- **position-σ growth**, and how often the position error exceeds 3σ;
- **ATE, and RPE over 1 s segments.**

| | V1_01_easy | MH_05_difficult | V2_03_difficult |
|---|---|---|---|
| frames (init) | 2907 (static) | 2267 (static) | 1889 (static) |
| ATE | 0.289 m | 0.755 m | 0.269 m |
| RPE, 1 s | 0.093 m | 0.246 m | 0.117 m |
| NEES / dof | **436** | **42.8** | **140** |
| NEES per block: attitude, position, velocity | 993, 3.96, 19.9 | 73.1, 55.2, 47.5 | 214, 9.71, 63.6 |
| NIS / dof | 1.51 | 2.16 | 14.7 |
| update leak ‖H₀N̂‖²/‖H₀‖²: max | 9.0e-32 | 6.5e-32 | 1.0e-31 |
| updates with a leak above 1e-6 | 0 of 35 686 | 0 of 27 915 | 0 of 16 265 |
| position σ, start → end | 0.18 → 0.26 m | 0.29 → 0.42 m | 0.17 → 0.25 m |
| position error beyond 3σ | 15 % of frames | 81 % | 50 % |

**What it says.** The filter is over-confident on all three sequences, worst in attitude on V1_01.
That is the #212 finding, now per sequence and per block. Across all 79 866 updates on three
sequences, **no camera update adds information along the unobservable directions**: the largest
share is 1e-31, rounding. That confirms [#480](https://github.com/branes-ai/cortex/issues/480) on
real data: the update is not where the filter learns what it cannot observe. The composition bench
reports S2's `observability.yaw_leak` (Φ against the yaw direction) at up to about 2e-6 per IMU
step, on synthetic and EuRoC loops alike. Over a run that is the remaining candidate, along with the IMU noise model.

### What the composition bench found

- **The S6d `nis.matches_direct` tolerance was too tight**, for the same reason as the symmetry
  check in #446. The filter forms its own S = H·P·Hᵀ + σ²I, and the two S's differ by rounding
  that scales with |H|·|P|·|H|ᵀ, not |S|. A captured loop showed 3.6e-15 against a 2.2e-15 bound.
  The tolerance now scales with the products.
- **#487 shows up in the loop too.** The built-in tapes use post-init frames 16–18; frames 13–15
  and 20–22 hold a diverging-ray track.

## The resulting workflow

```text
EuRoC run ──vio_pipeline --live-assert --capture cap/──▶ first violation: <stage>, frame F, <invariant>
     │                                                    + cap/<stage>/f<F>_<stage>_….json
     ├──<stage>_bench --fixture cap/<stage>/….json        (seconds, every type, every variant)
     │
     ├──vio_pipeline --capture cap/ --capture-loop F-2:F──▶ cap/C_filter_loop/loop.json
     │    c_filter_loop_bench --fixture …/loop.json        (the loop around it, every stage checked)
     │
     └──fix the stage in the filter (sdk/), re-run its bench and the composition bench, then
        confirm on the whole run: vio_trajectory_report (NEES, NIS, update leak, ATE/RPE)
```
