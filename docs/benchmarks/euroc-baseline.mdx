The goal for the VIO pipeline is accuracy competitive with OpenVINS on EuRoC.
Published tables use their own sequence subsets, start offsets, alignments and
evaluators, so a gap between our number and a paper's mixes the estimator with
the protocol. This baseline runs both estimators on the same data, through the
same evaluator, so the gap is the estimator's.

The numbers come from `scripts/euroc-baseline.json`, which
`scripts/euroc-baseline.sh` reruns and compares against (see
[the regression benchmark](#the-regression-benchmark)).

## Protocol

- **Data:** the 11 EuRoC MAV sequences, cam0 and imu0, ground truth from
  `state_groundtruth_estimate0`.
- **cortex** (commit 37bd455, 2026-11-07; every sequence changed from the first baseline at 348c774, see below): `vio_pipeline --source euroc` with
  its defaults, monocular, the MSCKF with double stages and a full covariance.
  Every run starts at the first frame.
- **OpenVINS v2.7:** the unmodified upstream binary, run outside every Branes
  repository with its upstream defaults, as [ADR 0002](../adr/0002-no-gpl-clean-room-policy.md)'s
  2026-11-06 amendment allows. Three run modes: monocular and stereo from
  the upstream start offsets, and monocular from the first frame. Only the
  aggregate metrics below are recorded. No test or CI job runs it or reads
  its output.
- **Evaluator:** `tools/traj_eval` scores every trajectory the same way. It
  matches each estimated pose to the nearest ground-truth sample within
  20 ms and reports:
  - **ATE 4-DoF:** translational RMSE after a position + yaw alignment, the
    four directions a VIO cannot observe. This is the headline number.
  - **ATE SE3:** the same after a rigid Horn alignment (`vio_pipeline`'s ATE).
    It also forgives roll and pitch errors, which a VIO can observe.
  - **RPE:** the error of the relative motion over sub-trajectories of 8, 16,
    24, 32 and 40 m of ground-truth travel. It is reported as the mean
    translation (m) and rotation (deg) error per length, then averaged over
    the lengths.

  The metrics are implemented in `branes::sdk::eval` from their definitions
  (Horn 1987 for the rigid alignment, and the closed-form yaw angle derived in
  `align_yaw`), and are unit-tested in `tests/sdk/trajectory_metrics.cpp`.
- **Window:** OpenVINS's upstream start offsets skip the opening seconds of
  the machine-hall sequences, so the **window** columns score cortex only
  from OpenVINS mono's first pose. Both estimators are then scored over the
  same span.

## What the baseline says

- **The typical gap is 2–3.5×** in 4-DoF ATE: the median over the 11
  sequences is 2.8×. V2_03, MH_05 and V2_01 are within 2×.
- **Initialization costs the most.**
  - MH_03 diverged in the first baseline (348c774: 29,804 m). Rotor vibration
    kept its still start out of static init, and the gravity-align fallback
    seeded a zero gyro bias, 0.08 rad/s off. Since #591 the fallback seeds
    the bias of the stillest vibrating rest window (error 0.004 rad/s), and
    MH_03 scores 0.484 m.
  - MH_01 starts moving, so static init cannot fire, and the first baseline
    seeded the filter from a 5-frame (0.25 s) dynamic alignment: 3.336 m over
    the whole run, 0.869 m over the window. Since #592 dynamic init waits for
    10 frames and a settled scale (the window's first 2/3 must agree with the
    whole within 5%), and a moving start waits up to 6 s for it rather than
    fall back at 2 s to gravity alignment with zero velocity. MH_01 then
    initialized 3.8 s after its first frame: 2.003 m over the whole run,
    0.580 m over the window.
  - The dynamic alignment's scale was 14% off at the median, mostly too
    small (97 accepted 1 s windows with prefer_dynamic over the first 30 s of
    the 11 sequences, against the ground-truth displacement). Fed
    ground-truth poses, the same alignment was 5–18% off, and about 2% once
    it also estimated the accelerometer bias. The error was the SfM track's
    positions: their noise attenuates an alignment that fits their second
    differences. Since #500 and #501 the window's gates come from the track
    noise, the window is bundle-adjusted, and the alignment estimates the
    accelerometer bias. On the same sweep 133 windows are accepted, and on the
    125 with ground truth the scale is 3.6% off at the median (12.8% at the
    90th percentile; 60% under 5%). On the 32 windows both versions accept
    (3 sequences), it goes from 13.5% to 2.8% (90th percentile 21.7% to
    9.9%).
  - In the default run that changes only MH_01 and MH_02, the two sequences
    that initialize dynamically: both now initialize at 0.5 s. MH_01 scores
    2.305 m over the whole run (2.19–2.41 m for a track noise of 0.8–1.2 px),
    MH_02 0.817 m. Neither is limited by its init: scored over their first
    20 s alone, both are within 2 cm of the truth from either init. MH_01's
    error grows while the drone rests on the ground from 20 s to 42 s: its
    ATE over the first 36 s is 1.27 m from the old init and 1.41 m from the
    new, and over the first 42 s 3.00 m and 3.35 m.
- **Rests drifted.** At rest a monocular MSCKF has no parallax to hold the
  velocity; on MH_01 the estimated speed grew from 0.06 to 1.6 m/s while the
  drone rested on the ground. Since #600 a zero-velocity update (S8) holds
  the velocity at zero and the gyro bias at the mean rate while both the IMU
  and the image are still. On these 11 sequences its detector fired at none
  of the 24,353 frames whose ground truth moves faster than 0.05 m/s (a
  measurement on these runs, not a guarantee). Over the whole run every
  sequence improves or holds: MH_01 2.305 → 0.322 m, MH_02 0.817 → 0.420 m,
  MH_04 0.481 → 0.376 m, V2_01 0.318 → 0.224 m, V2_02 0.410 → 0.331 m. Over
  the window MH_03, MH_04, MH_05 and V1_03 are 6–8% worse (MH_04 0.366 →
  0.396 m); the median ratio stays 2.8×. MH_05's rotation RPE over the whole
  run goes from 1.07° to 1.89°.
- **Rotation drift is the next gap.** In the Vicon rooms cortex's rotation
  RPE is 1.4–4.4× OpenVINS mono's (V1_02: 2.52° against 0.57°). That is
  consistent with the body-frame MSCKF's yaw observability leak (#212, #480).
- **Starting at the first frame matters for OpenVINS too.** Run monocular
  from the first frame, it diverges on MH_04 (4-DoF ATE 9,889 m) and is
  otherwise within 0.035 m of its start-offset score. Its start offsets hide
  the hard openings that cortex is scored on in the whole-run column.

## Front end or estimator?

From #595 to #601 the window's median ratio stayed at 2.7–2.8×, while the
whole-run error fell with each initialization and rest fix. To find which half
of the pipeline owns the remaining gap, `vio_pipeline --tracks gt` (#602) keeps
the real IMU and timestamps but replaces the KLT front end with ideal tracks:

- landmarks spawned along random pixel rays at 2–8 m (log-uniform) whenever
  fewer than 150 are in view, 15 px apart;
- projected through the ground-truth pose and the same cam0 calibration the
  filter uses, with Gaussian pixel noise of σ px;
- each track ends when its landmark leaves the image.

These are the tracks a perfect front end would deliver, with no outliers, drift
or calibration error. `--camera-sigma-px` sets the filter's visual noise `R`
(default 0.01 normalized, 4.6 px). The table gives window ATE 4-DoF divided by
OpenVINS mono's, with track seed 1:

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MH_01_easy | 3.5× | 1.3× | 7.9× | 11.3× |
| MH_02_easy | 2.2× | 0.6× | 2.8× | 5.9× |
| MH_03_medium | 3.0× | 2.1× | 2.4× | 2.7× |

- **The estimator owns the gap.** The comparison that isolates the front end
  keeps the filter's `R` at its default: with 1 px ideal tracks the median is
  2.4–2.8× over three seeds, against 2.8× for the real front end. A perfect
  front end at that noise would buy little. The noise-free column also
  changes `R`, so it is not a front-end comparison: it shows what the
  estimator reaches with perfect measurements.
- **The filter does not survive being told the truth.** With `R` matched to
  1 px ideal tracks, 3–5 of the 11 sequences diverge (worse than 10×),
  depending on the seed; with 0.5 px tracks and a matching `R`, 3–4 do (V1_01
  reaches 59–326×). A consistent filter improves when its measurements and
  its model of them both improve. This one gets worse: it takes in more
  information than the tracks carry and becomes over-confident. The default
  `R`, about 4.6× the KLT noise, is what keeps it stable. That points at the
  over-confidence described in #212 and in the rotation-drift finding above,
  not at the front end.
- **Even noise-free tracks leave 1.5–1.6×.** MH_02, MH_05 and V2_01 then beat
  OpenVINS mono's real-image score, but V1_01 stays at 2.3× and V2_03 rises
  to 6–9×.
- **Checked against artifacts:**
  - Ground truth and IMU agree in time to within 1 ms on V1_01, MH_01 and
    V2_01: the lag that best matches the gyro to the ground truth's
    differentiated rotation is 0.
  - Two real-image runs of V1_01 are bit-identical, so repeating a run
    measures no variation at all.
  - The spread across the three track seeds (medians of 2.4–2.8× at 1 px and
    the default `R`) shows how large a change a small perturbation alone
    produces.

## The consistency gate

The ideal-track split above showed that the body-frame filter gets worse when it
is told the truth about its measurements. `scripts/euroc-consistency.sh` (#605)
turns that into a pass/fail check that any backend can be run through:

- **One truth (#613).** Every input comes from a cubic B-spline through the
  ground truth (`vio_pipeline --imu gt`): the IMU is synthesized from it at the
  real IMU timestamps with the filter's own noise densities, the ideal tracks
  are seen from it with noise drawn in normalized coordinates (σ = S / fx, what
  a matched `R` says), and the run is scored against it (`traj_eval --truth`).
  `R` and `Q` are then the true noise. The bench is itself a
  [transformation graph](../methodology/euroc-consistency-gate.mdx).
- **Runs:** ideal tracks at S = 1 and 0.5 px, track seeds 1–3, on all 11
  sequences. Each runs twice: with the filter's `R` matched to S, and with its
  default `R` (4.6 px). Every backend uses the same, shipped S6d gate. That is
  132 runs of about 11 s each, since ideal-track runs read no images.
- **Pass condition** (bounds in `scripts/euroc-consistency.json`):
  1. no matched run is worse than 10× OpenVINS mono's window 4-DoF ATE (a run
     that writes no trajectory counts as diverged);
  2. at each S, the median matched ratio is below the median default ratio,
     and every default run was scored;
  3. **no yaw collapse:** nothing observes yaw, so the filter's world-yaw σ
     should not fall below its value at the gauge anchor. The median over the
     matched runs of (smallest yaw σ ÷ yaw σ at the anchor) must be at least
     0.5.

  A run that writes a trajectory that `traj_eval` cannot score is an
  evaluation fault, not a divergence. The gate then reports UNAVAILABLE, and
  `--record` refuses to store the result.
- **Pose NEES:** `vio_pipeline` scores every EuRoC run against the ground truth
  with `tools/pose_consistency.hpp`. It anchors the gauge (position and yaw)
  at the first scored frame, takes the error in the filter's own convention so
  its own covariance applies, and splits the attitude in the world frame. It
  writes the split and the world-yaw σ to `consistency.json`.
- **Why the yaw ANEES is not gated.** The first version of condition 3 was a
  median yaw ANEES of at most 3. Review of #607 showed that test is unsound.
  The yaw error is measured from the anchor, but the denominator is the
  current yaw variance alone. The variance of the change since the anchor also
  needs the anchor's variance and the covariance between the two times, which
  neither filter exposes. With a yaw prior the filter never reduces, the
  current variance mostly reflects the prior, so the ANEES reads low. The yaw σ
  ratio needs no cross-time term.
  - This replacement was made after the first results. The 10× and ANEES
    bounds had been set before any estimator ran.
  - The 0.5 bound lets the marginal yaw σ fall somewhat through its correlation
    with observable states (the R-IEKF reaches 0.52 on MH_03), but not
    collapse.
  - Every verdict below is the same at any bound between 0.13 and 0.9.

  and its yaw error does not shrink with it. This is the leak #480 attributed
  to S6 moving the mean.
- **The R-IEKF passes the two consistency conditions.** Its world-yaw σ holds
  at a median 90% of its anchor value and never falls below its 0.05 rad
  prior. At 1 px, matched `R` helps on 10 of the 11 sequences (the MSCKF: 4),
  and at 0.5 px the median is 1.25× OpenVINS mono's real-image score.

    velocity at the origin (see below).

    returns to it.

  R-IEKF scores 0.563 m on V1_01 against the MSCKF's 0.217 m, and 0.503 m on
  V2_03 against 0.178 m. With ideal tracks it is the better estimator. So what
  holds it back on real data is outside the estimator's consistency: its
  adapter still gates at 16 per dof instead of the χ² quantile (#481), seeds

  dynamic-init (#592, #597, #599) or zero-velocity (#600) work.

  accelerometer bias of 0.09–0.55 m/s² (V1_01: 0.548 m/s² on y). Both filters
  seed their tilt from the raw accelerometer, so they start 0.5–2.7° tilted
  and never learn the bias (V1_01: 2–3° throughout, with a filter tilt σ near
  0.06°). Even after the ground truth's own bias is removed, its body frame
  disagrees with the accelerometer's gravity by 0.1–0.9° at rest. That caps
  the roll/pitch NEES any filter can reach against it.

### Correction: the verdict was mostly the S6d gate

Running the R-IEKF through the shipped real-data path (#608) put it on the same
χ² quantile gate as the MSCKF, and it then failed conditions 1 and 2 as well:
9 of 66 matched runs diverge, and matched `R` scores 3.62× OpenVINS mono
against 2.45× for the default at 1 px (2.59× vs 2.18× at 0.5 px). Its yaw σ
ratio is 0.73, still a pass. Reverting one piece at a time on V1_01 at 0.5 px
puts the whole difference on the gate. The R-IEKF converges whichever init and
S8 it runs, as long as the gate is the old 16-per-dof line, and diverges with
the χ² quantile whichever init and S8 it runs.

The reason is that "matched `R`" is not the noise the filter sees. The ideal
tracks are synthesized from the ground-truth trajectory but the IMU is real, so
every observation also carries the ground truth's error relative to the IMU.
Even converged runs have NIS well above their degrees of freedom. A gate at
α = 0.05 assumes NIS ≈ dof, so it rejects a large share of valid long tracks,
and the filter's heading flips on what is left:

| Run (0.5 px, matched `R`), 4-DoF ATE (m), seeds 1 / 2 / 3 | χ² quantile, α = 0.05 | α = 10⁻³ | α = 10⁻⁶ | 5 per dof | NIS (5 per dof) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---:|
| R-IEKF, V1_01 | 99.4 / 4.7 / 83.4 | 0.14 / 0.16 / 0.16 | 0.13 / 0.14 / 0.13 | 0.12 / 0.13 / 0.13 | 1.65–1.67 |
| MSCKF, V1_01 | 9.0 / 22.7 / 4.0 | | | 0.14 / 0.16 / 0.16 | 1.65–1.67 |
| R-IEKF, V2_03 | 30.3 / 8.8 / 7.8 | 1.91 / 0.84 / 0.59 | 0.34 / 0.46 / 0.43 | 0.30 / 0.26 / 0.26 | 3.8–3.9 |
| MSCKF, V2_03 | 16.5 / 8.3 / 19.2 | | | 0.68 / 1.25 / 1.27 | 7.5–10.5 |

So conditions 1 and 2 above mostly compared gate lines. The pre-#608 R-IEKF
adapter gated at 16 per dof and the MSCKF at the χ² quantile. With the same
line the two converge to the same accuracy on V1_01. A difference survives on
V2_03, where the R-IEKF reaches 0.26–0.30 m against the MSCKF's 0.68–1.27 m with
half its NIS. The gate stays as recorded until #613 measures the ground truth's
residual floor, folds it into matched `R`, runs every backend with the same gate
line and re-records both. Condition 3 does not depend on the gate's line in the
same way, but it is re-measured there too. #613 did this by removing the floor
rather than modelling it: see [Results with one truth](#results-with-one-truth).

This is the fourth result in the VIO work that came from the test setup rather
than the estimator ([the storyline](../first-principles/storyline.mdx)).

## The R-IEKF on the shipped real-data path

Before #608 the R-IEKF reached EuRoC through a harness that gated at 16 per dof,
seeded a zero velocity from its own 150-sample static window, and had no S8. It
now runs the MSCKF's own initializer (static, vibrating-rest gyro bias,
gravity-align fallback, dynamic VI alignment), seeds its error state from that
seed's prior mapped into the right-invariant error, gates with the same χ²
quantile, and has the zero-velocity update. The change was planned as a
[delta on the OpenVINS transformation graph](../methodology/riekf-msckf.mdx).
`--riekf-legacy gate,init,zupt` puts any piece back, so each was measured on its
own, cumulatively, on the real KLT front end:

## The regression benchmark

`scripts/euroc-baseline.sh` reruns the baseline on the development station.
It runs `vio_pipeline` on every sequence, scores each trajectory with
`traj_eval` over the whole run and over the window, and compares the
whole-run scores with the recorded cortex numbers:

```sh
scripts/euroc-baseline.sh                    # all 11 sequences, ~25 min serial
scripts/euroc-baseline.sh V1_01_easy MH_05_difficult
CORTEX_BASELINE_JOBS=4 scripts/euroc-baseline.sh
scripts/euroc-baseline.sh --update           # record a new baseline
```

- **Verdict:** a sequence is **worse** when its 4-DoF ATE, RPE translation or
  RPE rotation exceeds the recorded value by more than 10%
  (`CORTEX_BASELINE_TOL`) plus a noise floor (5 mm, 5 mm, 0.05°). It is
  **better** when it falls below by the same margin.
- **Exit status:** the script exits non-zero on any worse or failed
  sequence.
- **Updating:** when a change improves the pipeline, `--update` records the
  new numbers and the commit; update the tables on this page to match.
- **Not in CI:** the script needs the EuRoC dataset on disk. The OpenVINS columns it
  prints are the recorded targets, not inputs; nothing here runs OpenVINS.

`CORTEX_VIO_ARGS` passes extra `vio_pipeline` flags to every run, for
example the ideal-tracks experiment above:

```sh
CORTEX_VIO_ARGS="--tracks gt --track-sigma 1 --track-seed 2" \
  CORTEX_BASELINE_OUT=build/ideal scripts/euroc-baseline.sh --no-build
```

`--update` refuses to record a run that sets it.

To score another estimator's trajectory on the same terms, write it as TUM
(`t x y z qx qy qz qw`, IMU pose in the world frame) and run
`traj_eval --dataset <mav0> --est <file> [--from <s>] [--json]`.
