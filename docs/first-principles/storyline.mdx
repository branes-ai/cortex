The chapters are in the order the problems were found. Each one names what the failure looked
like, the measurement that decided its cause, and the fix. Where a later chapter revised an
earlier claim, the revision is stated where the claim is.

## Chapter 1. Build it from parts

Epic <Issue id={33} /> built the pipeline bottom-up, each part unit-tested against synthetic
known answers:

- **Interfaces and backends:** the `VioBackend` interface, the MSCKF backend, a sliding-window
  skeleton.
- **Front end:** FAST-9 with non-maximum suppression, pyramidal KLT, images and pyramids
  ([S4](../pipeline/stages/s4-frontend.mdx)).
- **Models:** camera models with analytic Jacobians, IMU preintegration, feature representations
  with the MSCKF null-space types ([S0](../pipeline/stages/s0-sensor-models.mdx)).
- **Estimator:** static and dynamic IMU initialization, the state and propagator, the camera
  updater with null-space projection and a Mahalanobis gate, and the `VioEstimator` facade.
- **Evaluation:** EuRoC replay with ATE/RPE and a latency budget. A square-root covariance
  backend came later (<Issue id={187} />).

**When the epic closed, nothing had run on real data.** Its EuRoC acceptance tests skip in CI,
which has no dataset. Every later chapter is about what synthetic unit tests can't see.

- **Pitfall:** FAST-9 finds no corners on the X-junctions of an aligned checkerboard
  (<Issue id={37} />). Choose test images for the detector you have.
- **Pitfall:** the preintegrated Δv and Δp are gravity-free, with R_i·Δv = v_j − v_i − g·Δt
  (<Issue id={40} />). That convention became the seam of the worst bug in Chapter 2.

## Chapter 2. First contact with real data: 26 km of error

**Wrong extrinsics.** The first EuRoC V1_01 run gave an **ATE of 26,574 m** against a 0.5 m gate,
after a 13-minute run. There were two faults:

- The camera↔IMU extrinsics were left at identity. EuRoC cam0 is rotated about 90° and offset
  about 7 cm.
- Initialization fell back to an identity attitude when the start was never static.

The fix loaded the real T_BS, added `try_gravity_align` (roll and pitch from the mean specific
force), `InitDiagnostics`, a per-frame divergence guard and `--fail-fast`. V1_01 dropped to
**0.305 m**. The replay tests had the same identity-extrinsic fault: MH_05 was at 21,405 m and
V2_03 at 6,680 m until it was fixed (MH_05 **0.785 m**, <Issue id={245} />).

**The initialization race.** With real extrinsics, V2_03 still diverged to **12,456 m** with
`init=dynamic` (<Issue id={244} />). A degenerate dynamic init, whose scale wasn't observable,
won the race against static init. A scale-observability gate (`min_dynamic_motion` = 5 cm) made
dynamic init decline; static init then won, and the error fell to **0.293 m**.

**Forced dynamic init: a chain of faults** (<Issue id={247} />). Forcing the dynamic path on
MH_05 gave **41,910 m**, against 0.79 m with static init.

| Fault | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| F2 | Near-hover windows are rank-deficient, and the scale collapses to about 1e-8 | The observability gate; after it, dynamic init never fired |
| F4 | Bootstrapping SfM on adjacent 20 Hz frames gave almost no parallax: a best metric motion of **0.14 mm** | Use the widest-parallax pair: 56 mm, still 12.9 km of error |
| F5a, the root cause | The alignment solve used +g·Δt where the preintegrator uses −g·Δt, so it recovered g = −g_true. The seed was tilted **177.7°** from the accelerometer, injecting about 2 g of phantom acceleration | Fix the sign: tilt 177.7° → **2.33°**, error 48 km → **19.3 m** |

The synthetic fixtures carried the same sign error, so CI stayed green. Scoring ATE only after
initialization (19.9 m) confirmed the remaining 19 m is real post-init error.

A negative result: seeding roll and pitch from the accelerometer on a moving start made it worse
(**37.3 m**), because motion contaminates the specific force. It was reverted.

**Still open in initialization:**

- Forced dynamic init on MH_05 is still about 19 m against a 1.5 m gate (<Issue id={254} />). The
  start moves at about 0.1 m/s, and a 20% scale error over about 100 m explains it. This is an
  excitation floor, not a defect.
- A metric lever arm is added to up-to-scale SfM positions (<Issue id={499} />). Even on exact
  data the scale error is 0.88–5.49%, against 0.017–2.40% with the lever arm zeroed.
- The RANSAC inlier gates are about 0.9 px (two-view) and 2.3 px (PnP) (<Issue id={500} />). At
  2 px of noise no window ever builds. On MH_05, 82 of 170 moving attempts fail despite more than
  100 px of disparity. At rest, about 0.05 px of pseudo-parallax is accepted, because there is no
  minimum-parallax gate.
- Alignments with **21–90%** scale error are accepted, and up to 257% with 4 Hz keyframes
  (<Issue id={501} />). The gate checks observability, not consistency.
- Dynamic alignment declines in float and posit32, because it solves normal equations with a
  fixed ridge (<Issue id={468} />).
- The diagnostics log a 177° tilt but don't refuse the seed (<Issue id={267} />).
- Fixed: `jacobi_eigh` stopped only at an exactly zero off-diagonal and always ran 100 sweeps
  (<Issue id={497} />).

The full contract of this stage is on [S1 — Initialization](../pipeline/stages/s1-initialization.mdx).

## Chapter 3. Accurate, but over-confident

With initialization fixed, V1_01 met its ATE gate (0.305 m), but the relative error was about
**18%** (rotation 3.1°/m) against a 5% target (<Issue id={212} />). Once there were instruments to
measure it (NEES and NIS, gauge-anchored and per block, <Issue id={264} />), the real problem
showed: **the filter was over-confident.**

Measured in double (long double gives the same, so precision is not the cause; <Issue id={532} />):

| Sequence | ATE | NEES/dof | NIS |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1_01 | 0.289 m | **436** (attitude 993) | 1.5 |
| MH_05 | 0.755 m | **42.8** | — |
| V2_03 | 0.269 m | **140** | 14.7 |

Per-block NEES put the problem in **attitude** (V2_03: attitude 169, position 3.5). NIS stayed
healthy while NEES was in the hundreds: the innovations looked fine while the state covariance was
wrong. That combination is the signature of information leaking along an unobservable direction.

We built instruments to find the cause:

- **Stage probes**, synthetic and per stage, one per [pipeline stage](../pipeline/overview.mdx).
- **Real-data stage inspectors**, tapped from a live EuRoC replay (<Issue id={371} />).
- **An observability null-space audit** (<Issue id={337} />).
- **A per-stage ground-truth-injection harness** (<Issue id={266} />), later superseded by the
  probes.

Each candidate cause got a measurement. The method is described on
[Diagnostic Methodology](../pipeline/diagnostic-methodology.mdx), and the worked chain on
[Consistency Analysis](../pipeline/consistency-analysis.mdx).

| Candidate | Deciding measurement | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Q/R mistuned | Best NEES at Q×10. R×4 gives NIS 1.08 but ATE 2.07 m; R×10 gives 278 m. No (Q, R) pair is both consistent and accurate (<Issue id={280} />) | Refuted as the sole cause; tuning only compensates |
| Gross calibration or time-sync bias | Innovation mean −0.013σ | Refuted |
| Non-white innovations | After a deterministic update order, MH_05 innovations are white | Partly an artifact |
| **Yaw null-space leak (body-frame EKF)** | ‖H·N‖ is 2e-16 at a consistent point and **0.147** at σ = 2 cm, growing with σ (0.028 → 0.462); the translation leak is exactly 0 (<Issue id={337} />). On real V2_03 the mean leak is 0.39, nonzero on every update. The S6 update adds none (<Issue id={480} />); S2 leaks 2.4e-6 per 5 ms step | **Confirmed as a mechanism.** It lives in S2/S3 linearization, not S6. Its share of the NEES is not yet attributed |
| FEJ, measurement-only | Attitude NEES 993 → 1336 (V1_01); MH_05 → 107,544 and 20.9 m (<Issue id={339} />) | **Refuted** |
| FEJ, full | V1_01 → 2281 m; MH_05 → 11 km | **Refuted** as implemented: partial FEJ mixes a first-estimate H with a current-estimate P |
| Unmodeled calibration, as an isotropic R term | NIS 14.7 → 1.41, but NEES 140 → **3074** and ATE → 4.74 m | Refuted as the fix: it cures the symptom and worsens the state |
| Online spatial extrinsics (S10) | V2_03 NEES 140 → 110; NIS unchanged; ATE never better (<Issue id={332} />) | Cleared as the driver |
| Time offset t_d | not implemented | **Open** |
| Parallax gate (S5) | V2_03 ATE 0.27 → 1.99 m, NEES 140 → 322 | Refuted for the body-frame filter on EuRoC |
| Diagonal Q_d (S2) | Drops 0.35% of Q; propagation-only NEES 6.4 against 6 | Cleared for the body-frame filter (but see Chapter 5 for the R-IEKF) |
| Update algebra (S6) | NIS/dof 1.002 at matched (P, R); null-space residual 2.8e-16 | Cleared |
| Augmentation and marginalization (S3, S9) | Machine-zero residuals | Cleared |
| Arithmetic precision | Long double = double | Refuted |
| χ² gate too loose | 5·dof admits 59% (dof 1) and 70.5% (dof 3) of 2σ outliers, where χ²₀.₉₅ admits 44% and 22.5% (<Issue id={481} />) | **Open** contributor, never tested end to end |

**The structural cure: the R-IEKF** (<Issue id={347} />). A right-invariant error makes the
four-DoF gauge (translation and yaw) independent of the state, so the update cannot leak
information along it.

- **Proven at the unit level:** H·N = 0 and Φ·N = N at any state (<Issue id={348} />). On real
  V2_03 perturbations the yaw leak is **2.3e-16**, against 0.39 for the body-frame filter.
- **Assembled, it failed at first.** The backend diverged, and with updates it did worse than
  without them (162.9 m against 14.6 m, <Issue id={365} />). There were four bugs
  (<Issue id={366} />): the sign of H_φ, a retraction mismatch, a first-order Φ that needed to be
  second order, and a wrong NEES metric in the test. Only a multi-cycle finite-difference check of
  the joint step caught them.
- **Unit-proven is not system-proven.** On real EuRoC the R-IEKF still loses on accuracy: V1_01
  ATE 1.31 m against 0.29 m, V2_03 1.36 m against 0.27 m (<Issue id={429} />). Its attitude NEES on
  EuRoC has never been reported.

## Chapter 4. Divide and conquer, and numerics

Some numerical failures (a square-root ATE of 6e+40 m, a non-finite δx) could not be told apart
from algorithmic ones. That motivated the infrastructure of epic <Issue id={444} />, described in
its own [section](../debugging/overview.mdx):

- **Stages S0–S10 exposed as transformations**, so the backend is a call sequence.
- **Stage benches** with known-answer, ground-truth-injected and captured fixtures, checked by an
  invariant library with tolerances scaled to the arithmetic type.
- **Stream capture** from real runs with a first-violation trigger, and composition benches.

We then made the pipeline generic over arithmetic types:

- **Generic over types:** `StrictDouble` has explicit-only conversions, so an implicit narrowing
  is a compile error, and the result must match double bit for bit.
- **Precision measurement:** a precision mode measures the bits lost per type, and each stage can
  run in its own type ([Mixed Precision](../debugging/mixed-precision.mdx)).

What we learned:

- **Stages that write P (S2, S3, S9, S6e) must stay double**, unless the covariance is
  square-root (<Issue id={532} />).
  - Mechanism: in float, at takeoff (V1_01 frame 105), one update cuts the largest variance from
    1.49 to 0.135. The Joseph form's cancellation error (about ε·‖P_prior‖) then makes P
    indefinite (λ_min −4e-5 of the largest), and the run ends at 128,911 m.
  - A diagonal "undo negative variance" guard rejected 25,777 updates and still ended at 10,147 m:
    P is indefinite before any diagonal goes negative.
  - Square-root all-float holds all three sequences (V1_01 0.295, MH_05 0.784, V2_03 0.282 m).
  - Stages that only read P tolerate float.
- **Divergence follows conditioning, not the number type.** The S6e update loses log₂κ(S) to
  log₂κ(S)+3 bits in every type; data-movement stages lose none. posit16 is too narrow:
  triangulation breaks at 1° of parallax or less.
- **The square-root factor is tall after augmentation** (<Issue id={533} />, <Issue id={461} />).
  The update assumed a square factor and read past the end of H·S. That gave NaN on V1_01 in
  double, at a frame that varied from run to run (heap garbage), while synthetic tests passed bit
  for bit. After the fix, ASan and UBSan are clean on the real replay. The
  [worked example](../debugging/worked-example.mdx) tells how it was found.
- **Never re-factor P into S in a narrow type** (<Issue id={482} />). Clone/pose correlation puts
  pivots below float resolution: 36 of 51 were zeroed. Carry the factor instead.
- **Some tolerances should scale with ε and some must not**
  ([Numerical Thresholds](../debugging/numerical-thresholds.mdx)):
  - **Scale with ε:** the small-angle hand-off at max(1e-5, ε^(1/3)), and the PSD slack, measured
    against the largest P_ii rather than per entry.
  - **Never scale:** regularizing ridges stay at a fixed 1e-9. An ε-scaled ridge biased S1's float
    scale from 0.88% to 17.2% error, even in exact arithmetic.
- **Keep timestamps double.** Summing dt in float drifts about 6e-11 s per sample, so reset the
  filter clock from the sensor time after each propagation.

## Chapter 5. When the test lies

Three times, a "filter defect" turned out to be a defect in the test.

**The compiler was not the cause** (<Issue id={437} />). The R-IEKF end-to-end test passed on GCC
(3.44 m) and failed on Clang (58.48 m), with each compiler deterministic and the sanitizers clean.

- **Cause:** the test built the gyro and accelerometer samples from random draws inside two
  arguments of one call. Argument evaluation order is unspecified, so each compiler saw a
  different noise realization.
- **Fix:** sequence the draws. Clang CI now runs the whole suite.
- **The real lesson:** GCC's seed passed by luck. Sweeping 21 seeds showed 5 diverged (21–120 m).

**The world was wrong** (<Issue id={475} />, <Issue id={537} />). The R-IEKF looked inconsistent
and diverged on 5 of 21 seeds, and changing the χ² gate rescued some seeds while breaking others.
There were two causes:

- **The world:** the synthetic trajectory left its warm-up with a velocity step of about 1 m/s
  that no accelerometer sample recorded, while the ground truth reported rest. With **noise-free**
  sensors, roll/pitch NEES reached about 3000.
- **The adapter:** setting the state at t = 1.5 s still integrated the warm-up IMU samples from
  t = 0, a constant offset of about 3 m.

Smoothing the onset and dropping the stale samples fixed both. All 21 seeds then gave 1.8–7.6 cm
RMS at a normalized NEES of 0.56–1.84. The work also found one real filter gap: the process noise
wasn't right-invariant. A Monte-Carlo test 20 m from the origin showed the diagonal Q
under-predicted position variance **135×**.

<Aside type="caution" title="Revisions caused by the velocity step">
  - A synthetic "body-frame NEES ≈ 43 with perfect calibration", once read as reproducing the
    MH_05 over-confidence, came from the velocity step. It was retracted.
  - An explanation of a roll/pitch NEES of about 3400× as "over-fusion" was the velocity step too.
</Aside>

**A noise-free control proves nothing** (<Issue id={551} />). After the world was fixed, the
[S10](../pipeline/stages/s10-online-calibration.mdx) control read NEES 0.036, and the docs called
the body-frame filter "not over-confident". But that control fed **exact** observations. With
noise matched to what the filter assumes (1 px, the datasheet IMU densities) and perfect
calibration:

| 10 seeds | consistent | measured |
|---|---|---|
| pose NEES | 6 | 9.0–5088, median 26 |
| roll/pitch NEES | 2 | 2.9–129 (3–10 on 9 seeds) |
| yaw NEES | 1 | 3.0–4463 (8 of 10 above 6) |
| position NEES | 3 | 1.0–142 (7.5 or less on 9) |
| yaw σ, first → last frame | never falls | 0.16 → 0.006–0.020 rad |

Global yaw is unobservable, so its σ can only grow; here it falls about tenfold within seconds.
With IMU noise alone and exact observations, the yaw error reaches 0.22 rad on one seed, about
300× what gyro noise integrates to. This is the Chapter 3 yaw leak, reproduced for the first time
where the truth is known, and it needs no calibration error. The R-IEKF under the same noise gives
0.55–1.83. The body-frame filter was kept, characterized by a test, as the EuRoC accuracy
reference until the R-IEKF matches it there.

**Smaller ones:**

- The synthetic camera's tangential distortion p2 is 1000× EuRoC's (<Issue id={467} />). The
  world is self-consistent, so nothing failed.
- The S5 bench counts a correct triangulation rejection as a violation, so it becomes the first
  violation in every capture (<Issue id={487} />).
- The S6 inspector's "18% non-PSD" was its own positive-definite check, not a PSD check.
- Innovation non-whiteness came from the update order.
- Flight-data Allan variance overstates the noise density about 30×.
- The tools silently wrote nothing when `--out` didn't exist.

## Chapter 6. A slow start

With the world fixed, a slow motion onset (1 s or more) broke the R-IEKF again
(<Issue id={548} />): 14 of 21 seeds were out of band at 1 s and all 21 at 2 s, with roll/pitch NEES
up to 624.

- **Cause:** the R-IEKF triangulated with a linear solve only. At the 0.93° parallax of the first
  window (pixel noise 0.29°), all 234 features came out more than 1 m off (mean 5.6 m).
- **How it was confirmed:** substituting the true landmarks for the first second's updates
  brought roll/pitch NEES from 294 to 0.79.
- **Fix:** Gauss-Newton refinement of the reprojection error, which the body-frame path already
  had, with a backtracking line search that never accepts a step raising the cost. Onsets up to
  2 s are now in band on all 21 seeds; at 3 s one seed is marginal. Five iterations weren't enough
  from a seed that bad; 20 converge.
- **Why not a gate:** a parallax gate worked only in a narrow threshold window and threw away
  hundreds of tracks. On EuRoC V2_03 it had already regressed the body-frame filter.

The stage contract is on [S5 — Feature Triangulation](../pipeline/stages/s5-triangulation.mdx).

## What finishes the story

- The R-IEKF's NEES on real EuRoC (<Issue id={365} />) and its accuracy gap (<Issue id={429} />).
- Attributing the body-frame yaw leak to S2/S3 and sizing its share of the NEES (<Issue id={480} />).
- A χ²-quantile gate (<Issue id={481} />), and the time-offset state t_d, the one untested
  over-confidence candidate (<Issue id={332} />).
- The dynamic-initialization defects of Chapter 2.
- The FAST/KLT border mismatch (<Issue id={474} />) and real-time latency (<Issue id={213} />).
- Turning the real-data consistency checks from warnings into asserted bounds once a backend is
  consistent on EuRoC, and adding the [missing tests](regression-suite.mdx).
